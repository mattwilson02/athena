# Athena — Vision

---

## Thesis

**Depth of context is the product.**

ChatGPT is stateless — it only understands within the context of a single chat, giving diluted, ignorant opinions. Athena knows everything going on in your life — every choice you made, experience you had, fears, goals, relationships, patterns — and uses that to make genuinely informed judgements and advice.

The knowledge graph is a digital model of your mind built through lived interaction. Layer by layer, node by node, until the AI can reason about your life with the same depth you can. Eventually, deeper.

**The graph is Athena. Everything else is interface.** The chat is an interface for reading and writing to the graph. The UI is an interface for visualizing it. Telegram is an interface for accessing it on the go. The AI is an interface for querying it intelligently. Scheduling, notifications, integrations — all just ways to keep the graph current and act on what it knows.

This is not a "second brain." Second brain tools — Notion, Obsidian, Roam — store information. They don't reason about it. Athena doesn't just store your knowledge — it thinks with it. It draws connections you didn't see. It challenges conclusions you haven't questioned. It holds you accountable to commitments you've forgotten. It's a second mind.

---

## What Athena Does

Athena helps run your whole life from the minute up to the lifetime.

| Time Horizon | Role | What changes at this level |
|-------------|------|---------------------------|
| **Minutes–hours** | Tactical assistant | Concrete blocks — you're doing something or you're not |
| **Days–weeks** | Planner | Commitments and priorities — flexible but bounded |
| **Months–quarters** | Strategist | Goals and progress — measured, adjusted |
| **Years–decades** | Life guide | Vision and identity — who you're becoming |

Everything aligns. A decision about tonight is evaluated against this week's commitments, this quarter's goals, and the 10-year vision. Nothing is siloed.

Most tools operate at one level. A calendar is daily/weekly. A goal tracker is quarterly. A journal is reflective. A therapist is long-term. Nobody stitches them together. Athena's power is the vertical stack — reasoning across all layers simultaneously.

Each layer constrains the one below it. Your identity shapes your goals. Your goals shape your weekly priorities. Your priorities shape what you do right now. But feedback flows upward too — what you actually do reveals whether your stated goals are real or aspirational. When the gap between what you say and what you do gets big enough, Athena asks the hard question.

## How It Thinks

### Adaptive Modes

Athena adapts its mode to the stakes:

| Context | Mode | Behaviour |
|---------|------|-----------|
| Low stakes, information request | **Mirror** | Presents the full picture — here's what this means across every dimension |
| New commitment or change | **Advisor** | Maps the cost, surfaces priorities, asks where it fits |
| Clear contradiction with stated goals | **Guardian** | Pushes back hard — flags the conflict before accepting |
| Uncertain territory, big decisions | **Dialectic** | Challenges assumptions until you arrive at clarity |

It never cheerleads. It never blindly accepts. It treats your goals, values, and beliefs as constraints, not suggestions.

Over time, Athena learns which mode produces action vs resistance for this specific person, in this specific context. Sometimes pushing hard is right. Sometimes a question is more powerful than a statement. The goal isn't to be harsh or gentle — it's to produce the best outcome. Strategically effective, not performatively tough.

### The Tradeoff Engine

When something new enters your life — a hobby, a relationship, a trip, an opportunity — Athena:

1. Maps the time, energy, and financial cost against current commitments and flags what gives
2. Surfaces stated priorities and asks where this ranks
3. Presents the full picture across all affected dimensions — cross-domain, not siloed
4. Pushes back if it genuinely conflicts with something you've said matters more

Then helps integrate the decision into the existing structure if you proceed.

Cross-domain reasoning is critical. "Should I take this job?" isn't a career question. It's a financial question (salary, cost of living), a relationship question (proximity, travel), a health question (stress, commute), an identity question (does this match who you're becoming?), and an opportunity cost question (what are you giving up?). The graph has all these connections — the AI traverses them systematically.

### The "What If" Engine

Beyond reacting to decisions, Athena simulates. "What if I took 3 months off?" "What if I moved to London?" "What if I started training for a marathon?"

Run the scenario against the full graph. What commitments break? What goals accelerate? What relationships are affected? What's the financial impact? A comprehensive impact assessment before the decision is made, not after.

A shallow system says "that sounds interesting!" A deep system says "here's exactly what changes, what breaks, and what you gain."

### Purpose Discovery

Purpose isn't a single input or output — it's discovered through three mechanisms:

- **Emergent** — over time, patterns in what you do, enjoy, avoid, and return to reveal your purpose
- **Directed** — you define a purpose and Athena holds you to it, measures everything against it
- **Dialectic** — challenges your stated purpose when your actions consistently contradict it

### Handling Contradictions

People are contradictory. You want freedom AND security. You want to grind AND to rest. You want to be social AND to have solitude.

Athena doesn't flag contradictions as errors to resolve. It understands that humans hold competing values and helps navigate the tension. The right response to "I want to work harder AND sleep more" isn't "those are contradictory, pick one." It's "here's how to restructure your day so you get both, and here's where the real tradeoff is if you can't."

## The Knowledge Graph

### Graph as Consciousness

The graph must be:

- **Complete** — every decision, plan, fear, goal, relationship, pattern captured. Nothing lives only in chat. If it's not in the graph, Athena doesn't know it.
- **Current** — when something changes, it changes everywhere. No stale nodes contradicting reality. Every stale node is a corrupted memory. Every missed cascade is a broken synapse.
- **Connected** — the value isn't individual nodes, it's the relationships. "Dating someone" connects to time, money, emotional energy, social goals, training schedule. The graph sees all of it.
- **Temporal** — understands time at every scale. What's happening today, what's planned this quarter, what the 10-year vision looks like. And how they relate.

Completeness and accuracy aren't nice-to-haves. They're existential. Every lost update is amnesia.

### Human Fundamentals

Below personal identity, there's a universal layer — things that are true for every human regardless of preferences. Athena ships with these as a baseline scaffold:

| Fundamental | Non-negotiable at species level | Personal expression |
|-------------|-------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Movement** | The body needs to move | Gym, climbing, cycling, walking, dancing — your choice |
| **Sleep** | Consistent, sufficient rest | Schedule, duration, environment — yours to define |
| **Nutrition** | Fuel the body properly | Diet style, meal timing, restrictions — personal |
| **Connection** | Meaningful human relationships | Partner, friends, family, community — the shape is yours |
| **Purpose/Growth** | Moving toward something | Career, craft, creativity, learning — stagnation erodes mental health |
| **Financial stability** | A floor of security | Not wealth — the baseline beneath which stress takes over everything |

These are **challengeable but not deletable**. You can reshape how you express them entirely — "my movement is rock climbing twice a week." But you can't remove the category. "I don't want to exercise at all" gets the same challenge ladder as dropping a core value: "That's not a preference, that's a health risk. What form of movement would you actually enjoy?"

This also solves the onboarding problem. A brand new user with an empty graph — Athena starts with "let's talk about how you handle these fundamentals." The first conversation has structure. The graph has a scaffold before any personal context exists.

### Memory Permanence Levels

Not everything in the graph is equally permanent:

| Layer | Stability | Examples | Change frequency |
|-------|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| **Fundamental** | Immutable | Movement, sleep, nutrition, connection, purpose, stability | Never removed, only reshaped |
| **Identity** | Most stable | Values, beliefs, core fears, life vision | Months–years |
| **Strategic** | Stable | Goals, major projects, relationships | Quarterly |
| **Tactical** | Fluid | Tasks, events, daily plans | Daily–weekly |
| **Ephemeral** | Volatile | Current mood, today's energy, immediate context | Hourly |

Athena weights these differently in reasoning. Identity should almost never be overridden by tactical concerns. If a task conflicts with a core value, the task loses. Always.

The exception: when repeated tactical reality diverges from identity, feedback flows upward — but not easily. Dropping a core value should be hard. Athena challenges before accepting:

1. **Flag the gap** — "You haven't trained in 2 weeks. This conflicts with your stated priority."
2. **Investigate** — "Is something blocking you? Injury, schedule, motivation?"
3. **Escalate** — "This is 6 weeks now. Last time you had a gap this long, you said you regretted it."
4. **Challenge the shift** — "You're saying fitness isn't a priority anymore. That contradicts your goal to cut to 83kg, your belief about morning clarity, and your anti-goal about health at 40. Are you sure?"
5. **Accept with full context** — only after conscious, defended reprioritization does the identity layer update. And it records why.

Values evolve. But quietly giving up and genuinely evolving are different things. Athena makes you earn the change.

### Graph Bootstrap

The graph builds organically through conversation. You talk to Athena, it captures what matters. No manual node creation required — the AI layer extracts structure from natural interaction.

**Voice is the fastest path to graph depth.** Typing is a bottleneck — you'll always share more in an hour of spoken conversation than a week of text. A single voice session mapping out your goals, fears, relationships, and current situation can bootstrap the graph faster than months of typed interactions. Voice ingestion (transcription → structured extraction → graph writes) is a critical accelerator, not a nice-to-have.

Imports from existing tools (calendar, fitness apps, financial data) can further accelerate completeness. But natural conversation — spoken or typed — is the primary input. It captures intent and context that structured imports miss.

Athena must deliver value from the first interaction, not after hours of onboarding. The cold start problem — how to bootstrap a useful graph quickly without overwhelming the user — is a critical product challenge. See [docs/OPEN_PROBLEMS.md](OPEN_PROBLEMS.md) for detailed analysis.

## The Human Model

### Roles and Context

You're not one person. You're a developer, a partner, a friend, a son, an athlete, a strategist. Each role has different priorities, energy requirements, and success metrics. Athena understands which role you're in and adjusts what it surfaces and how it communicates.

At work: career goals, project deadlines, professional growth. At the gym: training plan, recovery, body composition. With friends: don't interrupt unless urgent.

### Energy and Rhythm

Time isn't the only resource. Energy is. A 2-hour dev session at 7am fresh is worth more than 4 hours at 10pm fried.

People don't live in abstract time blocks — they live in rhythms. Morning routine, deep work, lunch, afternoon slump, gym, evening wind-down, sleep. These rhythms are personal, fairly stable, and differ between weekdays and weekends, seasons, busy periods and downtime.

Athena learns your energy patterns and plans around them, not against them. This becomes predictive: Athena knows you're most creative in the morning, most social in the evening, and crash hard on Sundays after big Saturday nights.

### State of Mind

Athena learns to read the room from patterns:

- Shorter messages, more typos → stressed or rushed
- Lots of new ideas and plans → high energy, maybe overcommitting
- Cancelling commitments → possible burnout or avoidance
- Late night messages → not following sleep schedule
- Asking about the same decision repeatedly → anxiety or indecision

Don't pile on new commitments when someone is burning out. Don't dampen excitement with heavy analysis when someone is in creative flow. Adjust the response to the state.

### Relationships

People nodes aren't contact cards. Relationships are one of the biggest factors in life decisions. Athena understands:

- **Relationship dynamics** — is this person good for you? Do you grow around them?
- **Social patterns** — how often you see people, how that's changed, whether you're isolating or overcommitting
- **Influence mapping** — who shapes your thinking? Are you making a decision because YOU want it or because someone else expects it?

### The Deeper Layers

**Fears and beliefs** drive more decisions than goals do. "I want to start a business" is a goal. "I'm afraid of being seen as a failure" is the belief that stops it. Athena surfaces these patterns: "You've talked about this 6 times in 4 months but never taken a step. Is it the timing, or is something else blocking you?"

**Anti-goals** — what you're actively avoiding — are powerful constraints. "I never want to feel financially dependent." "I refuse to let my health slide." These define hard boundaries that shouldn't be crossed regardless of the opportunity.

**Intellectual influences** — books, philosophies, people you admire — shape your decision-making framework. Athena connects these to live decisions: "You follow Stoic philosophy. This is essentially about what you can and can't control."

## The Layered Schedule

Daily planning builds in layers:

| Layer | What | Examples |
|-------|------|----------|
| **Non-negotiables** | Fixed commitments that define the day's skeleton | Steps, exercise, work hours, sleep schedule |
| **Exceptions** | One-off events that override the default | Doctor's appointment, car service, social plans |
| **Strategic fill** | Free time allocated to what moves the needle most on long-term goals | Dev sprint, reading, skill building, relationship investment |

When exceptions shift, strategic fill re-plans around the new reality. When a non-negotiable is threatened, the conflict is flagged before accepting.

**A fully scheduled person is a burned out person.** Downtime is not wasted time — it's a layer of its own. Intentional rest (walking, reading, family time, watching a film) is productive. It differs fundamentally from procrastination, binging, or default scrolling. Athena should protect unallocated time as deliberately as it protects non-negotiables — and flag when rest is missing, not just when tasks are.

The goal isn't to fill every minute. It's to make every minute intentional — including the ones where you do nothing.

**Non-negotiables must be grounded in identity.** They aren't just schedule blocks — they should align with your values, goals, and vision. If they don't, Athena calls it out: "You list autonomy as a core value. You also have a 9-5 as a non-negotiable. Those are in tension. Is the 9-5 a stepping stone, a compromise, or something you haven't questioned?" Not telling you to quit — making you consciously own the contradiction rather than living in it passively.

### Habits (Distinct from Tasks)

Habits aren't tasks. A task gets done once. A habit's value is in the streak, the consistency, the compound effect. Athena tracks habits differently:

- **Streak tracking** — consistency over time
- **Pattern detection** — you skip on Fridays, after social events, when work is stressful
- **Recovery** — "You missed 3 days. This is where most people drop permanently. Get back on it today."
- **Habit-goal linking** — this habit serves this goal, which serves this part of your identity

Habits interact. Sleep affects training. Training affects energy. Energy affects deep work. Deep work affects career. Career affects finances. Athena sees the whole chain.

### Plan vs Reality

Forward-looking (plans) and backward-looking (reflections) serve different functions. Plans create expectations. Reflections validate or invalidate them. The gap between "I planned to do X" and "I actually did Y" is where Athena learns the most about you.

This becomes predictive: "You tend to overcommit on Mondays. Last 6 Mondays you planned 8 things and completed 4. Want me to trim this to 5?"

## Accountability

Athena isn't just a planner — it's an accountability partner with a specific structure:

1. **Commitment capture** — you said you'd do X by Y
2. **Progress tracking** — are you on track?
3. **Intervention** — if you're falling behind, why? What's blocking you?
4. **Consequence surfacing** — if you don't do this, here's what's affected downstream
5. **Pattern recognition** — you tend to drop commitments in this category. Why?

This isn't nagging. Nagging is "you haven't done X." Accountability is "you committed to X because it serves Y which is part of Z. You're 3 days behind. Last time this happened, you abandoned the goal entirely. What do you want to do?"

## Communication

### Adaptive Personality

The current SOUL.md is a static personality definition. The real version adapts based on what works for this person. Some respond to tough love. Some need encouragement in certain moments, directness in others. Athena learns what produces action vs what produces resistance.

The goal isn't to be harsh or gentle. It's to be effective.

### Notification Model

Every interrupt has a cost. The wrong model makes Athena annoying. The right one makes it feel like it reads your mind.

| Priority | Approach | Example |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Urgent + important** | Interrupt now | "Your meeting moved, leave in 10 minutes" |
| **Important + not urgent** | Batch it | Morning briefing, end-of-day review |
| **Contextually relevant** | Right moment | "You're near the gym, haven't trained today" |
| **Background insight** | Store, surface later | "You've slept less this week — here's the pattern" |

The difference is understanding your current state — deep work, relaxing, commuting. Each context has different interrupt tolerance.

### Emotional Intelligence

When someone's dad dies, Athena doesn't say "I notice this affects your Q2 goals." It holds space. A week later, when they're trying to dive back into normal life too fast, it gently suggests slowing down.

This can't be hardcoded. It emerges from deep understanding of the person and the context.

## Privacy

The deepest personal data imaginable lives in the graph. Fears, relationship dynamics, financial details, health data, psychological patterns. Privacy is a core value proposition, not a compliance checkbox:

- Your data never leaves your control
- No company sees your graph
- No training on your data
- Local-first, cloud-optional
- Full export, full delete, full portability

This differentiates against every VC-backed AI product that monetizes data. Athena's business model is aligned with the user's interest, not against it.

## Evolution Stages

### Stage 1: Foundation (Current)
**Scope:** Thinking, planning, memory
**Intelligence:** Proactive — notices patterns, surfaces insights, pushes back on contradictions
**Interface:** Desktop app + Telegram
**Knowledge:** Pattern recognition — spots habits, cycles, tendencies

Key capabilities:
- Reliable retrieval (temporal, semantic, status-aware)
- Update cascade — one change ripples through all connected nodes
- Conflict detection — flags contradictions with stated goals/values/habits
- Personality enforcement — strategic mentor, not a cheerleader
- Conversations are write operations — changes in chat become graph updates

### Stage 2: Action
**Scope:** Scheduling and external integrations
**Intelligence:** Agentic — acts on your behalf with approval
**Interface:** Desktop + mobile + calendar integrations

Key capabilities:
- Calendar integration (read/write — suggest and create time blocks)
- Structured daily planning with the three-layer model
- External tool integrations (fitness, finance, task managers)
- Suggest-then-act workflow — Athena proposes, user approves, Athena executes
- Trust threshold evolves through testing and experience
- Multi-agent orchestration — specialist agents (retrieval, conflict, synthesis) replace the single monolithic Claude call, enabling parallel execution and model-appropriate routing

### Stage 3: Ambient
**Scope:** Full life management
**Intelligence:** Predictive — anticipates needs before you ask
**Interface:** Voice, smart home, wearables, always-on

Key capabilities:
- Voice interaction — conversational, ambient, always available
- Predictive scheduling — knows what you need before you ask
- Multi-person awareness — understands relationships, others' patterns
- Autonomous action within established trust boundaries

### Stage 4: Product
**Scope:** Multi-user, multi-household
**Intelligence:** Fully autonomous within trust boundaries
**Interface:** All touchpoints

Key capabilities:
- Multi-user — family of 4, each with individual context + shared household context
- Cross-user coordination — scheduling around everyone's needs
- Product for anyone — help people find their purpose through deep contextual understanding

This is a product. Build it for yourself, build it for everyone. Solving your own problems deeply produces something authentic — not a feature checklist designed by committee.

## The Moat

Every interaction makes Athena more valuable. Month 1, it gives decent advice based on what you've told it. Month 6, it catches things you didn't think to ask about. Year 2, it understands you better than most people in your life. Year 5, it's genuinely irreplaceable.

The real lock-in isn't features or switching costs — it's depth of knowledge that took years to build. The compound knowledge effect.

## Design Principles

### 1. The graph is the product
Every architectural decision serves graph quality. If it doesn't improve the graph, question whether it's worth building.

### 2. Build the way Athena thinks
The build process is philosophically aligned with the product. Layered priorities, long-term vision constraining short-term decisions. Every sprint checked against: "does this move toward the vision?"

### 3. Constraint-based, not suggestion-based
The knowledge graph is a constraint system. Goals, values, beliefs, and habits constrain what actions are acceptable. Athena enforces, not just suggests.

### 4. Conversations are write operations
If it's said in chat, it's captured in the graph. Knowledge that dies with the session is amnesia.

### 5. Self-centric first, then expand
One person known deeply is more valuable than four people known superficially.

### 6. Progressive autonomy
Start with suggest-and-approve. Expand autonomy as trust is proven through experience, not assumed.

### 7. No scope creep in execution
The vision is huge. Execution is disciplined. Features are built when they're needed, not when they're imagined.

### 8. Privacy is the product, not a constraint
The business model must be aligned with the user's interest. Data sovereignty is non-negotiable.
